# Synthetic Conditional Allocation — Sanity Experiment v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-28  
**Code:** `experiments/synthetic_conditional_allocation_reference.py` (self-contained, seeded NumPy)  
**Status:** Executed synthetic mathematical illustration; **NOT a result on Sepsis Challenge patient records and NOT clinical evidence**.

## Experiment question

Can a *time-legal*, informative two-level context yield a conditional blend that outperforms both a training-selected constant mixture and the global Brier/moment allocation? Can a random, uninformative context do the same? What happens to positive-patient and tail losses?

## Synthetic design

- 3,000 independent synthetic patient sequences × 12 time steps. Training patients: 1,800; validation patients: 600; never-used-before test patients: 600. All splits are patient-disjoint by construction.
- The binary event is sampled from a hidden probability generated by a negative baseline logit, a patient-level severity factor, and a deterministic time pattern. Simulated hourly prevalence and patient-positive share do not claim to reproduce real cohorts.
- Two simulated probability experts use the same latent logit plus differing amounts of independent Gaussian logit noise. In regime 0, static is less noisy; in regime 1, temporal is less noisy.
- The **regime is observable before prediction** and independent of the outcome-generation noise. This is a deliberately favorable, identifiable example: in actual clinical telemetry, the true error regime is not known.
- Training: choose constant `q` on a grid `[0,1]` by patient-average Bernoulli log loss; fit regime-specific Brier moment weights with ridge shrinkage toward that constant.
- Validation: choose ridge strength from five predeclared values. Test: evaluate frozen policies once, use paired patient bootstrap (1,500 replicates), no tuning on test.
- Negative control: shuffle context assignments, fit two groups and choose shrinkage by validation as above; such a context has no systematic informational relationship to expert quality.

- Constant blend weight `q = 0.500`; global Brier moment weight `q = 0.499`.

- Test-positive patient share: 0.195 (retrospectively determined, not a controller input).
- Paired mean patient-log-loss improvement vs. constant: **+0.006495**; exploratory patient bootstrap percentile 95% interval **[+0.004191, +0.008847]**.
- Exact sample Brier-gain polynomial identity verified numerically within `1e-12`.

## What this demonstrates—and what it does not

**Demonstrates in a deliberately constructed synthetic setting:** If a time-legal feature clearly identifies which expert has lower predictive noise, conditional weighting can outperform a global constant mixture. The uninformative shuffled context does not show the same large advantage. Global moment optimization and a strong constant blend can be nearly identical, as predicted by the earlier analytical equivalence.

**Important counterpoint:** The conditional policy improved average patient loss and CVaR90 yet *slightly worsened mean loss among patients who had any positive label* relative to the constant blend. Thus, even a favorable mean improvement does not make the policy universally better for the rare-outcome group. This subgroup is retrospective evaluation only: **event status cannot define a live controller regime**.

**Does not demonstrate:** that our actual HMM, missingness, information value, real covariance estimates, feedback gate, or Sepsis model have predictive context strong enough to reproduce this result; a causal benefit of measuring additional vitals; calibrated confidence bounds; clinical utility; or a genuine effectiveness guarantee. The data-generating assumptions are intentionally favorable and do not model all correlated real errors, informative missingness, patient outcomes, or deployment shift.

## Transfer into the real retrospective benchmark

1. First test `patient_mean_log_loss` and all tail metrics in the registry using synthetic fixtures.
2. Freeze real static and temporal expert predictions. Use **training-only out-of-fold predictions** to estimate moments and select a constant reference; do not fit covariance on the never-seen test cohort.
3. Pick **one** context candidate from permitted telemetry (e.g. freshness or expert disagreement), define regimes *before outer evaluation*, fit/shrink on training, select thresholds on validation.
4. Run patient-disjoint outer folds, then final holdout only after policy freeze; bootstrap full patients. Include an uninformative-context permutation control with the permutation procedure predetermined.
5. Compare mean patient log loss, calibration, CVaR90, positive-patient loss, paired gain/win rate, and fallback frequency. Keep static or constant if tail harm or confidence is unacceptable.
6. Never claim that the synthetic proof establishes the clinical usefulness of conditional adaptation.
